# Objection Handling

Use these responses on calls, in email replies, and on discovery calls. Acknowledge first, then reframe. Stay calm and consultative — you're a partner, not a vendor pushing gym classes.

---

## "We already do resident events."

**Acknowledge:** "That's great — it sounds like resident experience is already a priority."

**Reframe:** "What we typically see is that teams run strong seasonal events — pool parties, holiday gatherings, move-in welcomes — but consistent weekly wellness programming is harder to sustain because it requires instructor coordination, scheduling, and follow-through every week."

**Question:** "Is the challenge more about consistency, or about the team's bandwidth to run it?"

**Close:** "ResideWell handles the weekly wellness layer so your team can keep doing the events you're already great at — without adding another hat. Would an 8-week pilot to test that split be worth exploring?"

---

## "We do not have budget."

**Acknowledge:** "I hear that — budget is tight everywhere right now."

**Reframe:** "That's exactly why we structured an 8-week pilot at $4,000 instead of leading with an annual contract. It's designed to be a low-risk test you can expense as resident experience or marketing — often less than a single resident event with catering."

**Question:** "Is it a timing issue — nothing until next fiscal year — or is resident programming not prioritized at all?"

**Close:** "If timing is the issue, I can send materials for your next budget cycle. If it's about proving value first, the pilot is built for that. Which is closer to your situation?"

---

## "Residents may not attend."

**Acknowledge:** "That's a fair concern — and honestly, the wrong programming model fails for that reason."

**Reframe:** "We address attendance with three things: class variety matched to your resident demographic, consistent scheduling so residents can plan, and registration plus reminders so it's not a 'show up and hope' model. We also track attendance every week and report back — so you're not guessing."

**Question:** "Have residents asked for specific types of classes — yoga, Pilates, strength?"

**Close:** "The pilot is 8 weeks specifically so we can measure real attendance before you commit further. If it doesn't work, you've spent $4,000 to learn — not $20,000 on a year-long contract. Does that de-risk it enough to try?"

---

## "We already have a gym."

**Acknowledge:** "Your fitness center is clearly an asset — and for a lot of residents, that's enough."

**Reframe:** "ResideWell isn't a gym replacement. We're an amenity activation layer. Many Class A properties have beautiful gyms that peak in January and go quiet. Live classes — especially in a studio, rooftop, or lounge — give residents a reason to use spaces they walk past every day and create community that a treadmill can't."

**Question:** "Do you have other spaces — a yoga room, courtyard, or resident lounge — that are underused compared to the gym?"

**Close:** "We'd use your existing spaces, not build new ones. Worth 15 minutes to see if that activation angle fits [Property Name]?"

---

## "We need corporate approval."

**Acknowledge:** "That makes sense — a lot of our partners are part of larger management companies."

**Reframe:** "We can arm you with a one-page pilot overview, sample reporting, and a clear scope of work so you have everything needed for an internal conversation. We've also found that framing this as resident retention and amenity utilization — not 'fitness' — helps with corporate reviews."

**Question:** "Who typically signs off on resident experience pilots — regional, VP of operations, or marketing?"

**Close:** "Would it help if I joined a brief call with your regional contact, or would you prefer to champion it internally first? I'm happy to support either way."

---

## "Send me information."

**Acknowledge:** "Absolutely — happy to."

**Reframe:** Don't let this end the conversation.

**Action:**
1. Send pilot one-pager within 2 hours
2. Include 3 bullet points tailored to their property in the email body
3. Propose a specific follow-up date in the same email

**Close:** "I'll send the overview today. I also included a few ideas specific to [Property Name]'s [space]. Would [date] work for a quick 15-minute call after you've had a chance to review?"

---

## "We are too busy right now."

**Acknowledge:** "I completely understand — property teams are stretched thin, which is actually why we exist."

**Reframe:** "The whole point of ResideWell is that we don't add to your plate. You approve a schedule, share a link, and we handle instructors, setup, registration, and reporting. Most managers tell us it takes less than an hour a week of their time."

**Question:** "Is 'busy' a timing issue — check back in 30 days — or is resident programming just not a priority right now?"

**Close:** "If it's timing, I'll follow up in [month]. If it's priority, I'll step back. Which would you prefer?"

---

## "We tried fitness classes before and they did not work."

**Acknowledge:** "That's really helpful to know — and unfortunately pretty common."

**Reframe:** "When we dig in, it usually failed for one of three reasons: inconsistent schedule, wrong instructor fit for the residents, or no one owned promotion and registration so residents didn't know about it. We built ResideWell specifically to solve those three failure points."

**Question:** "Do you remember what went wrong — was it attendance, instructor quality, or the team having to manage it?"

**Close:** "The 8-week pilot is structured to test whether a managed model works differently. If the answer is no, you've learned something valuable. If yes, you've got data for a longer program. Open to a short call to compare notes on what didn't work before?"

---

## General Principles

1. **Never argue.** Acknowledge, reframe, ask a question.
2. **Use their property name and amenities** — generic responses feel like spam.
3. **Pilot de-risks everything.** $4,000 for 8 weeks is your best tool.
4. **You're not selling gym memberships.** You're selling resident experience, retention, and amenity ROI.
5. **Log objections in CRM Notes** — patterns will inform your pitch over time.
